# KEY ACCOUNT MANAGEMENT RISK — COMBINED MEMO TO CEO
**Date:** 05 June 2026
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team
**Subject:** Single-point-of-failure risk across top two sales representatives (Ian & Peter)

---

## Executive Summary

A review of the customer database (2,273 records) has identified that the business's
**two largest sales representatives — Ian and Peter — together manage 441 customers
(19.4% of the total customer base) across 53 combined regions**, with no documented
succession or backup arrangement in place.

Both reps carry serious account management risk. The nature of that risk differs
between them, and each requires a tailored response from the CEO.

## Ian — Risk Profile

**232 customers | 31 regions | South-East corridor specialist**

Ian is the business's single largest sales representative by customer count.
His portfolio spans 31 regions — the widest spread of any rep in the business —
covering the South-East corridor: Enugu, Delta State, Rivers/PH, Edo/Benin,
Imo State, Anambra State, and Akwa Ibom, among others.

**Why Ian poses the highest succession risk:**

- His portfolio has **no dominant anchor region** — his largest region (Enugu)
  accounts for only 22% of his accounts. This means if Ian were to leave,
  the CEO would need to redistribute customers across 31 different regions
  simultaneously, with no single obvious successor.

- Despite geographic coherence (largely South-East states), **13 of his 31 regions
  hold only 1 customer each.** These lone customers are the most likely to churn
  if contact with Ian is disrupted — they have no relationship depth with the business
  beyond one individual.

- At 232 accounts, Ian's workload is **unsustainable for a single person.** Service
  quality for customers outside his core five regions (Enugu, Delta, Rivers, Edo,
  Imo) is likely already thin.

**Recommended actions (Ian):**

1. Assign a deputy or junior rep to shadow Ian across his core South-East regions,
   building independent customer relationships now rather than after a departure.
2. Immediately reassign the 13 single-customer outlier regions to geographically
   closer reps with available capacity.
3. Cap Ian's active portfolio at a manageable ceiling (suggested: 150 accounts)
   and redistribute the remainder to a new or existing South-East rep.

---

## Peter — Risk Profile

**209 customers | 22 regions | South-West base with scattered outliers**

Peter is the business's second-largest rep and the most geographically scattered.
While his portfolio is anchored in Ibadan/Oyo (115 customers, 55%), the remaining
45% is spread thinly across 21 other regions — including Kaduna, Enugu, Ghana,
Benue State, and Uyo — which have no logical connection to his South-West base.

**Why Peter poses a serious but different risk:**

- **Over-concentration in one region:** 115 of Peter's 209 accounts sit in
  Ibadan/Oyo. This makes Peter the de facto owner of the business's largest
  market. If Peter leaves or becomes unavailable, the Ibadan market — the
  single biggest customer hub — loses its primary relationship manager overnight.

- **Incoherent geographic spread:** Peter's 13 single-customer outlier regions
  span the country with no pattern. These accounts are almost certainly
  receiving poor attention given the travel and time required to maintain them.

- **Territory overlap:** Several of Peter's outlier regions (Kaduna, Enugu,
  Ogun State) are already well-covered by other reps (Stephen, Ian, Tope).
  This creates confusion about account ownership and duplicated effort.

**Recommended actions (Peter):**

1. Immediately identify a successor or backup rep specifically for the
   Ibadan/Oyo cluster (115 accounts) — this is the business's most
   important single territory.
2. Reassign all 13 single-customer outlier regions to the geographically
   nearest active rep. Peter should not be managing accounts in Ghana,
   Kaduna, or Benue.
3. Define a formal territory boundary for Peter: South-West only
   (Ibadan, Ogun State, Osun State, Lagos overflow).

## Combined Business Impact

If both Ian and Peter were unavailable at the same time (illness, resignation,
or dispute), **441 customers across 53 regions would be unmanaged.** That is
nearly 1 in 5 customers in the database with no active sales rep contact.

The financial and reputational risk of this scenario is significant enough to
warrant immediate structural action.
